# Queue Burst and Cancellation QA — 2026-10-25

Every tested burst completed without exceeding four active payloads. Priority
ordering, cancellation of queued clients, SIGKILL cleanup of holders and
subsequent queue recovery passed.

## Method

The real buildq CLI at `3a9e793108ddf1c3bebbd4d1af87cacf0b7ff79f` ran short Python
children from eight local Git directories, with pooling disabled and
`kill_tree=true`. Each child wrote a 4 KiB file, slept for 120 ms, and appended
start/end events under an OS file lock. Events, rather than status polling alone,
established peak active-payload concurrency.

This is a **queue contention test**, not a Rust compiler benchmark. Results below
use the debug binary during other QA activity on the shared 4-CPU machine. Wall
and CPU figures therefore describe that run, without establishing a performance
ranking for polling settings or production release builds.

## Results

| Clients | Poll interval | Batch wall time | Child CPU time | Peak active payloads | Largest observed status latency |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 250 ms | 0.529 s | 0.353 s | 4 | 0.010 s |
| 32 | 250 ms | 3.058 s | 1.598 s | 4 | 0.008 s |
| 128 | 250 ms | 16.827 s | 13.496 s | 4 | 0.074 s |
| 8 | 10 ms | 0.437 s | 0.394 s | 4 | 0.008 s |
| 32 | 10 ms | 1.682 s | 2.305 s | 4 | 0.037 s |
| 128 | 10 ms | 15.252 s | 17.962 s | 4 | 0.683 s |

All 336 burst jobs exited zero. Every batch had matching start/end events and
history records, and left no tickets behind. More aggressive polling did not
remove contention at 128 clients in this run.

With all slots held, eight queued jobs with priorities 0/1/5 started in the exact
priority-then-FIFO order shown by status. Separately, 32 queued jobs were submitted,
16 were cancelled with SIGTERM/SIGKILL, and both active holders were SIGKILLed.
No cancelled waiter started. All 16 surviving waiters and a new recovery job exited
zero. Known holder children were no longer running; no busy slots, waiters or
tickets remained. Recovery after killing holders took about 0.99 seconds.

These SIGKILL checks do not cover the distinct SIGTERM build-script defect
demonstrated in [the mbx/lifecycle report](qa-2026-10-25-mbx.md) and
[#111](https://github.com/r4ai/buildq/issues/111).

## Release Confirmation

A second run used the release binary with other compilation stopped. Every
burst again had matching successful exits/events/history, peak payload
concurrency four, and zero remaining tickets. Priority and cancellation/recovery
checks also passed. These are single-run observations, not confidence intervals.

| Clients | Poll interval | Batch wall time | Child CPU time | Largest observed status latency |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 128 | 250 ms | 15.220 s | 9.623 s | 0.033 s |
| 128 | 10 ms | 8.485 s | 13.622 s | 0.492 s |
| 128, four direct workers | No buildq queue | 5.279 s | 5.175 s | Not sampled |

The direct control invokes the identical Python payload through four workers;
its first event was 17.78 seconds after the last release queue experiment event.
It provides a diagnostic lower bound rather than equivalent durability,
priorities, watchdogs, history or pooling. Aggregate child CPU includes payloads,
watchers and status sampling. The measurement does not attribute the full gap
to one queue operation. Smaller polling improves wall time here while increasing
CPU and status contention; the measured improvement opportunity is tracked in
[#116](https://github.com/r4ai/buildq/issues/116).
